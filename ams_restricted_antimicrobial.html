Access antibiotics have a narrow spectrum of activity, lower cost, a good safety profile and generally low resistance potential. They are often recommended as empiric first or second-choice treatment options for common infections.
Watch antibiotics are broader-spectrum antibiotics, generally with higher costs and are recommended only as first-choice options for patients with more severe clinical presentations or for infections where the causative pathogens are more likely to be resistant to Access antibiotics, such as upper urinary tract infections (UTIs).
Reserve antibiotics are last-choice antibiotics used to treat multidrug-resistant infections.

PROCEDURE:
  1. The attending/service physician shall fill out this form completely upon ordering for the specific restricted antibiotic/s in the patient's chart.
  2. Approval from the Department Training Officer or Chairman for orange-coded antimicrobials and AMS Team Leader, ID consultant and Department Chairman or Consultant on-duty (if ID consultant and AMS Team Leader are not available) for the red-coded antimicrobials, may be via phone/online platform consultation or actual rounds. If by phone or online platform, the referring resident shall sign in the form and document date and time of the call. The approving physician should sign in the designated area on the form within 24 hours to document his/her agreement.
  3. Pharmacy will dispense the ordered doses of any of these restricted antibiotics within 24 hours (48 hours if ordered on weekends), provided the attending/ service physician has filled out this form at the time he/she ordered the said antibiotic in the patient's chart. Succeeding doses after the 24th hour (48th hour if ordered on weekends) will already need the phone, online platform or written approval as mentioned in number 2, for the pharmacy to dispense the ordered drugs.
  4. The pharmacists shall check for the availability of this completed form before serving the ordered antibiotic/s.
